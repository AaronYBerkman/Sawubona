# Drafts: asking the two dictionaries for permission

Sawubona is built from two SASL dictionaries' public videos. The videos stay on
the machine that built the app; what ships is numbers derived from them, never
the videos: the sign models' embeddings of each clip, and - for Real SASL - the
tracked positions of each presenter's body, hands and face outline over time
(`data/replay.bin`, about 15 MB), which the app draws as an illustrated signer
with facial features. Say all of that plainly when asking. Both sites reserve
their rights, so ask before giving the app to anyone else. Adjust the bracketed
parts, keep it short, and be ready to take the data out if they say no.

---

## 1. Real SASL

Send via https://www.realsasl.com/contact.

**Subject:** Permission for a free SASL practice tool built from your dictionary

Hello,

I have built a small, free practice tool for [name], who is learning South
African Sign Language at [course]. It runs entirely on their laptop: they sign a
word at the webcam, and it says which sign in your dictionary it most resembles,
then links to your page for that sign.

To do that I downloaded your public sign videos and turned each one into
numbers describing the movement: the tracked positions of the body, the hands
and the outline of the face over time. The tool contains those numbers and an
illustrated figure drawn from them, including facial features (brows, eyes,
nose and lips), which replays each sign so a learner can watch it and see their
own attempt beside it. It does not contain your videos, and every sign links
back to realsasl.com.

Because the figure follows each presenter's face as well as their hands, I want
to ask about that specifically: are you, and the signers in your videos,
comfortable with their face movements being drawn this way? If not, I can leave
the face out of the drawing, or take the replay out altogether.

May we use it this way, for personal study, and share it with [a few fellow
students / the class]? It is not for sale and never will be. If you would rather
we didn't, I will remove everything derived from your videos. If you have a
preferred way of providing the data, or want credit shown differently, I am glad
to follow it.

Thank you for documenting SASL so carefully.

## 2. National Institute for the Deaf (NID)

Send via https://nid.org.za/contact.

**Subject:** Permission for a free SASL practice tool using your dictionary's free clips

Hello,

I have built a small, free practice tool for [name], who is learning South
African Sign Language. It runs entirely on their laptop: they sign a word at the
webcam and it tells them which sign it most resembles.

It works much better for new signers when each word has more than one signer, so
it now uses the clips of the whole free tier of the SASL Video Dictionary on
learnsasl.com, about 5,900 words, and nothing beyond it. Each clip was turned into
numbers describing the movement - the tracked positions of the signer's body,
hands and face outline - and the tool contains those numbers, not your videos.
From those positions it also draws an illustrated figure signing the word, so a
learner can watch the movement; the figure is a drawing made from the numbers,
not a frame of the video.

The tool's fingerspelling reader also learned from your video "The A–Z of
Fingerspelling": from the hand positions tracked in it, every example checked by
hand. Fifteen of its 24 alphabet drawings are traced from those positions - line
drawings, not frames of the video - and credited to it in the app.

May we use them this way, for personal study, and share the tool with [a few
fellow students / the class]? It is not for sale. If not, I will remove
everything derived from your clips. We would also be glad to credit NID in the
app, and to hear whether you would support wider use, for example for learners
on NID courses.

Thank you for the dictionary. It is an extraordinary resource.

[Name]
[Email]
